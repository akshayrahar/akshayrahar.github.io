Academic Research

Dissertation research

Leeds University Business School · 2024
Investigating the carry-over effects of research & marketing intensities on firm performance in the automobile industry

Analysed a 23-year longitudinal dataset (2000 to 2023, publicly traded U.S. automobile firms, pulled from COMPUSTAT North America via WRDS. Fixed-effects panel models to strip out firm-level factors that do not change over time. Hausman test confirmed FE was the right call over random effects. Marketing intensity shows a positive carry-over effect at t and t-1, then reverses at t-2. The takeaway: marketing spend compounds for roughly two years before returns start to fade.

About

A bit more about me

My work sits somewhere between performance marketing and analytics. I run campaigns, fix the tracking when it is wrong, and use the data to decide what to scale, what to stop, and what to test next.

I started in B2B market research, then moved into paid media and analytics. Built Google Ads and Meta accounts from the ground up, fixed broken GA4 setups, and built dashboards that replaced manual reporting for clients. The client mix has been e-commerce, B2B SaaS, and media.

The MSc at Leeds was proper quantitative work: panel data, econometrics, fixed-effects models on 23 years of real firm data. That changed how I think. Now when I look at campaign data, I check whether the numbers are actually reliable before I act on them. I don'ust pull the numbers, I interrogate whether they're reliable before I act on them.

Based in the UK. Open to hybrid and remote. Most interested in performance, analytics, or growth roles where the data side and the execution side are expected to work together.
