I started in B2B market research, then moved into paid media and analytics. Built Google Ads and Meta accounts from the ground up, fixed broken GA4 setups, and built dashboards that replaced manual reporting for clients. The client mix has been e-commerce, B2B SaaS, and media.

Research

Do Marketing & R&D Budgets Compound? A 23-Year Study

Leeds University Business SchoolMSc Dissertation · 2024 · Distinction

3,163 firm-year observations. Fixed-effects panel models. 23 years of U.S. automobile company data from COMPUSTAT North America via WRDS. Two research questions: do marketing intensity and R&D intensity each carry over into future firm performance — and for how long?

The research question

Most attribution treats spend and outcome as roughly simultaneous. This dissertation tested whether marketing and R&D investment each have a lagged carry-over effect on gross profit margin, using publicly traded U.S. auto firms across 2000–2023. The automobile sector was chosen because it is both marketing-intensive (Ford alone spent ~$4bn in 2021) and R&D-intensive (~$138.7bn globally in 2022 across the sector), making the carry-over question practically significant for capital allocation decisions.

The method

  • Dataset: 3,163 firm-year observations, U.S. publicly traded automobile firms, 2000–2023 (COMPUSTAT North America via WRDS).
  • Dependent variable: Gross Profit to Sales ratio — chosen over net income for stability and because it isolates operational performance from financial engineering.
  • Independent variables: Marketing Intensity and R&D Intensity, each lagged at t, t-1, and t-2.
  • Control variable: Firm size (number of employees) to account for scale differences across firms.
  • Model: Fixed-effects panel regression to strip out time-invariant firm characteristics that could confound results.
  • Model selection: Hausman test confirmed Fixed Effects over Random Effects (p<0.05).
  • Robustness: Alternative lag structures and subsample sensitivity analysis — core results held.

What the results showed

Marketing intensity: Positive and significant at t (β = 0.55, p<0.05) and at t-1 (β = 0.27, p<0.05) — confirming a carry-over into the following year. At t-2 the effect reverses sharply (β = -2.52, p<0.05). Marketing spend generates positive carry-over for up to one year, then inverts strongly at two years — consistent with advertising wear-out effects documented in the literature. The t-2 reversal is nearly five times the magnitude of the positive t coefficient, so this is a reversal, not a fade.

R&D intensity: Negative and significant at t (β = -0.30, p<0.05), t-1 (β = -0.20, p<0.05), and t-2 (β = -0.12, p<0.1). R&D expenditure consistently compressed gross profit margins in the auto sector across the full two-year window. This is consistent with R&D costs being expensed immediately against revenue while the innovation benefits (new models, patents, platform launches) materialise over a longer horizon not captured in a two-year lag.

The contrast between the two variables is itself a finding: marketing spend generates near-term carry-over then sharply reverses; R&D costs weigh on margins throughout the window. Both findings support long-term thinking over short-term budget cuts in either area.
