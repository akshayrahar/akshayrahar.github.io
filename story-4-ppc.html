How We Dropped Cost-Per-Lead by 52% in 6 Weeks , Without Increasing Budget

A B2B client was paying £148 for every lead from Google Ads. Industry benchmark: £68. The campaigns weren't broken , they were just unmeasured, unsegmented, and quietly bleeding spend on keywords that hadn't converted in six months. Here's the full teardown.

Weekly Cost-Per-Lead across the 10-week period(4 weeks baseline, 6 weeks post-optimisation. Budget held constant at £3,200/month.

The Starting Point: A Campaign Running on Autopilot

The client had been running Google Search Ads for 14 months. The account had been set up by an agency, handed over mid-contract, and had essentially been running on auto-bidding ever since. Nobody had touched the negative keyword list in seven months. The search term report was a graveyard of irrelevant queries consuming real budget.

The brief was simple: reduce CPL without touching the budget. The implication was that either the targeting was wrong, the bids were wrong, the landing page was wrong, or some combination of all three.

The answer, as it turned out, was all three , but unevenly distributed.

£148
Starting Cost-Per-Lead. With a monthly budget of £3,200 and an average of 22 leads/month, the unit economics made the channel barely viable. The client needed CPL under £80 for the channel to be profitable.

The Audit: Where Was the Money Going?

Before changing anything, I ran a full spend audit across three dimensions: what keywords were triggering ads, how budget was distributed across campaigns, and what the post-click experience looked like. The results were illuminating.

Spend Audit , Week 1
Budget Allocation vs. Conversions by Campaign Type

Brand and competitor campaigns consumed only 18% of budget but delivered 61% of all conversions. Generic broad match campaigns consumed 52% of budget and generated just 12% of conversions.

The data told a clear story: the account was spending backwards. The highest-intent campaigns , branded terms and competitor conquesting , were budget-capped and frequently hitting daily limits before noon. Meanwhile, broad match generic campaigns were burning through budget on queries like "what is B2B software" with zero commercial intent.

The Three-Layer Fix

Layer 1 , Search Term Hygiene (Week 1-2)

Downloaded 6 months of search term data and ran it through a relevance filter: any query that had spent over £15 with zero conversions in 90 days was added to the negative keyword list. The audit surfaced 214 negative keywords that had never been excluded , including entire categories (jobs-related queries, academic research terms, competitor brand names being bid on accidentally).

Within 72 hours of implementing negatives, the impression share on converting queries rose from 41% to 67%. The same budget was now visible to higher-quality audiences more often.

Search Term Quality
Converting vs. Wasted Spend , Before and After Negative Keyword Cleanup

214 negative keywords added across 4 campaigns. Wasted spend (zero conversions after £15+ spend) fell from 38% of total budget to 8% within two weeks.

Layer 2 , Budget Reallocation (Week 2-3)

Using the conversion data from the audit, I restructured budget allocation across campaigns. Brand and competitor campaigns , which had been capped at £18/day , were increased to £34/day. The broad match "awareness" campaign, which had been consuming £38/day with almost no conversions, was paused entirely and its budget redistributed.

Budget Restructure
Daily Budget Allocation: Before vs. After Restructure (£/day)

Total daily budget held constant at £106/day (£3,200/month). Allocation shifted from awareness-heavy to intent-heavy, with brand and exact match receiving the largest increases.

Layer 3 , Landing Page Alignment (Week 3-6)

The final layer was the hardest to fix but had the biggest impact: message mismatch. Every ad , regardless of the search intent , was sending traffic to the same generic homepage. A user searching "B2B CRM software pricing" and a user searching "CRM demo request" both landed on a page that opened with a 400-word brand story and a newsletter sign-up form.

Working with the client's developer, we built two dedicated landing pages: one for evaluation-stage queries (pricing, comparison, features) and one for decision-stage queries (demo, free trial, get started). Each page matched the ad's message precisely and had a single, prominent CTA.

"The ads were reaching the right people. The landing page was then talking to completely the wrong version of them. Message match is the silent conversion killer."
Conversion Rate by Landing Page
Landing Page CVR: Homepage vs. Dedicated Pages (Post Week 3)

Homepage CVR: 1.4%. Evaluation landing page: 3.8%. Decision landing page: 6.2%. The decision-stage page alone drove 44% of all conversions in weeks 4-6 despite receiving only 28% of traffic.

The Results: 6 Weeks, Same Budget

Across all three optimisation layers, the combined effect on CPL was significant. The improvements compounded , better negative keywords meant higher quality score, which reduced CPC; better budget allocation meant more impressions on high-intent terms; better landing pages meant more of those impressions converted.

£71 CPL
Final Cost-Per-Lead after 6 weeks , down from £148. That's a 52% reduction. Monthly leads increased from 22 to 45. Same £3,200 budget. The channel went from barely viable to the client's highest-ROI acquisition source.

The Broader Lesson

PPC optimisation is almost never about finding a magic keyword or writing a better headline. It's about removing the drag , the wasted spend, the misaligned destinations, the budget flowing to campaigns that can't convert. Once you stop the bleeding, the efficiency gains appear naturally.

The three-layer framework I used here , search term hygiene, budget reallocation, landing page alignment , applies to virtually any Google Ads account that's been running for more than 90 days without a structured audit. The specific numbers change. The principle doesn't.

  • Start with the search term report, not the keywords tab. What are people actually searching before they see your ad?
  • Follow the conversion data to rebalance budget, not gut feel about which campaigns "feel" important
  • Match the message to the moment. One landing page for all traffic is almost always leaving conversion rate on the table
  • Wait 2-3 weeks before judging any change. Google's smart bidding needs time to recalibrate to new signals , premature reversals are one of the most common optimisation mistakes
