Your GA4 is Missing 34% of Conversions. Here's Why

A client was convinced their Google Ads weren't working. The conversion rate looked terrible. The real problem? Their GA4 setup was invisible to nearly a third of the funnel. Here's exactly what we found and how we fixed it.

Tracked vs. untracked conversion events across the funnel. Before the fix

The Problem Nobody Could See

The brief was simple: a B2C client was spending £4,200/month on Google Ads and seeing a 1.9% conversion rate in their GA4 dashboard. The account manager was pushing to pause campaigns. The data said they weren't working.

Before touching the campaigns, I ran a full tracking audit. What I found stopped the conversation entirely.

34%
of conversion events were never being sent to GA4, completely invisible to every report and campaign decision made over 4 months.

What a Tracking Audit Actually Looks Like

Most GA4 implementations are assembled in layers , some tags are from a developer, some from a marketer using GTM, some legacy Universal Analytics carryovers. Each layer introduces potential for events to fire incorrectly, fire twice, or not fire at all.

Using GA4 DebugView, browser DevTools, and a cross-referenced Tag Assistant session, I mapped every event across three key stages:

Funnel Stage Analysis
Tracked vs. Missed Events Per Funnel Stage (Before Fix)

Each bar shows total expected events vs. events actually reaching GA4. The checkout stage had the largest gap , a misfired dataLayer push was silently swallowing purchase confirmations.

The Root Cause

Three specific issues were responsible for the 34% gap:

  • Purchase confirmation fires before page load completes , the GTM tag triggered on DOM Ready but the purchase dataLayer push happened after an async payment confirmation. Events were firing into a void.
  • Duplicate GA4 configuration tags , the client had migrated from UA but kept a GA4 config tag in both GTM and hardcoded in the site header. Some hits were being counted, others blocked by deduplication filters.
  • Form submission events misattributed , a contact form's generate_lead event was only firing on the thank-you page, but the page was served via JavaScript redirect , meaning the pageview (and event) never loaded for ~23% of mobile users.
Impact Measurement
Monthly Conversions: Reported vs. Actual (Before & After Fix)

After implementing the fix across three GTM triggers, actual monthly conversions jumped from 28 (reported) to 74 (actual, recovered). The campaigns had been working all along.

"The campaigns weren't failing. The measurement was. Fixing the data revealed a 2.6× higher conversion rate than anyone knew existed."

The Fix: Three Changes, Permanent Visibility

1. Trigger Refactor , Window Loaded + dataLayer Listener

Moved purchase event triggers from DOM Ready to a custom dataLayer event listener (gtm.custom.purchase_complete), allowing the async payment confirmation to push data before the tag fires. This alone recovered 71% of the missing purchase events.

2. GA4 Configuration Deduplication

Removed the hardcoded GA4 snippet from the site header and centralised all configuration through a single GTM-managed GA4 Config tag. Hits immediately normalised , no more split tracking.

3. Form Submission , SPA-Compatible Tracking

Replaced the page-load-dependent form trigger with a click-based listener on the submit button, using eventCallback to delay the redirect until after the GA4 hit confirmed delivery. Mobile tracking went from 77% to 99.1%.

+164%
increase in measured conversions, not from changing ads, not from increasing budget. From fixing the measurement layer that was already there.

What This Means for Every Analytics Setup

The uncomfortable truth is that most GA4 implementations have gaps. They accumulate during migrations, CMS updates, developer handoffs, and A/B test launches. The average e-commerce GA4 setup I've audited is missing between 18% and 41% of its intended events.

The fix is almost never glamorous , it's trigger logic, dataLayer structure, and a systematic QA process. But the impact on every subsequent decision , campaign pausing, budget allocation, channel attribution , is enormous.

Before you optimise your ads, validate your tracking.
