Do Marketing Budgets Compound? A 23-Year Study

📍 Leeds University Business School 📅 2024 · MSc Consumer Analytics and Marketing Strategy 🏷 Econometrics · Panel Data · COMPUSTAT · R² = 0.96

3,163 firm-year observations. Fixed-effects panel models. 23 years of publicly traded U.S. automobile company data. The question: does marketing spend in year one still show up in your gross profit in year two or three?

The Question

Most marketing attribution treats spend and outcome as happening at roughly the same time. But marketers often argue that brand investment takes time to pay off. This dissertation tested whether that is actually true, using real financial data rather than surveys or case studies.

The specific focus was the automobile sector, where marketing budgets are large, brand reputation matters significantly, and the data is publicly available through COMPUSTAT North America via WRDS.

The Method

  • Dataset: 3,163 firm-year observations from publicly traded U.S. auto firms, 2000 to 2023.
  • Dependent variable: Gross Profit to Sales ratio (chosen because it isolates operational performance and is less volatile than net income).
  • Independent variables: Marketing Intensity and R&D Intensity at time t, t-1, and t-2.
  • Models: Fixed-effects panel regression to control for firm-level characteristics that do not change over time.
  • Model selection: Hausman test confirmed Fixed Effects over Random Effects.

What the Results Showed

Marketing intensity at time t: positive and significant (β = 0.55, p < 0.05). In plain terms, higher marketing spend correlates with higher gross profit in the same year.

Marketing intensity at t-1: positive and significant (β = 0.27, p < 0.05). The effect carries over into the next year.

Marketing intensity at t-2: negative and significant (β = -2.52, p < 0.05). By two years out, the effect reverses.

The short version: marketing investment compounds over roughly two fiscal years, then the carry-over effect fades. This has direct implications for how firms should think about budget cuts — pausing spend in year one shows up in performance in year two.

Why It Matters for Marketing Practice

Most PPC and paid media work focuses on what happens this month or this quarter. That is necessary. But the dissertation showed that brand and marketing spend has a tail that extends beyond the immediate period, and cutting it has consequences that do not show up in next month's report. They show up 12 to 24 months later.

That is a useful thing to be able to demonstrate with actual data when a client or CFO asks why marketing budget should not be cut in a slow quarter.
